**Leading v0.4 theorem.** Let q ≥ 2^18 = 262144 be an integer. Every finite simple bipartite graph G on n ≥ 2q vertices, with maximum degree at most 2q − 2 and

$$
e(G)\ge(q-1)n-q^{3/2}/128,
$$

contains a nonempty q-regular subgraph using original vertices and edges. The power is interpreted over the reals; the equivalent integer allowance is floor(q^(3/2)/128).

**Parameterized theorem.** For integers h ≥ 6 and q ≥ 4096(h + 1)², the same conclusion holds with the same order and degree-cap assumptions when

$$
D=(q-1)n-e(G)\le hq-16h^2.
$$

The deficit D is signed integer subtraction in the parameterized statement and signed real subtraction in the fractional-power statement. The degree cap makes it nonnegative. The total vertex-degree shortfall from 2q − 2 is 2D, not D. Neither theorem asks the caller for minimum degree, balanced shores, a critical core or successful cuts. Neither promises spanning, induced or connected support, or transfers the earlier 4q − 6 support guarantee.

Formal endpoints: `R898/Main.lean:Erdos585.R898.superlinear_forcing` and `R898/Main.lean:Erdos585.R898.every_h_forcing`.

Labels used in this note: H49 is the 3q − 32 theorem (q ≥ 16384), H4 the 4q − 44 theorem (q ≥ 25600), H5 the 5q − 58 theorem (q ≥ 36864) and L34 the 2q − 5 theorem (q ≥ 5). Identifiers such as R898 name source folders, not theorems.

At q = 2^18, the real allowance is 4q; the parameterized statement with h = 7 allows 7q − 784. The real formula is implied by the parameterized theorem at every q ≥ 2^18 (take h = floor(√q/64) − 1, so the allowance is about q^(3/2)/64 for large q); for 2^18 ≤ q ≤ 409576 it is also weaker than the retained D ≤ 5q − 58 theorem.

## 1. The classical feasibility step

Fix balanced shores A,B and define

$$
k(P)=|A\cap P|-|B\cap P|,\qquad
 g(P)=e(A\cap P,B\setminus P)-qk(P).
$$

In the usual integral-flow network, the source-to-A and B-to-sink capacities are q, and original A-to-B edges have capacity one. The cut with source side {s}∪P has capacity q|A|+g(P). Thus a spanning q-factor exists exactly when g(P) ≥ 0 for every P. This is classical; Bollobás, Kim and Verstraëte use this capacity network in the proof of Theorem 2.2 of their February 21, 2003 public manuscript. [Author manuscript, pp. 3–4](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~jverstra/regular.pdf).

For fixed a∈A,b∈B, the inequalities

$$
q(\mathbf 1_{b\in P}-\mathbf 1_{a\in P})\le g(P)\quad\text{for every }P
$$

are exactly the factor criterion with demand zero at a,b and demand q elsewhere. Deleting a,b therefore gives a q-factor on the remaining shores. The proof's job is to find a suitable support and, when needed, one pair satisfying every inequality simultaneously. A factor criterion on a supplied support alone does not do that work. The balanced specialization of Enomoto, Ota and Kano's Lemma C is another expression of the classical criterion. [Published original, 1988](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.3190120115).

## 2. Deriving a critical support

**Lemma (critical-support normalization).** Let $h\ge6$, $q\ge4096(h+1)^2$ and $F_0=hq-16h^2$. If a host satisfying the parameterized theorem above contains no nonempty $q$-regular subgraph, then some original vertex set $S$ carries a simple bipartite subgraph $K$ with

$$|S|>2q,\qquad q\le d_K(v)\le2q-2\quad(v\in S),\qquad
(q-1)|S|-e(K)=F_0,$$

and

$$(q-1)|U|-e(K[U])\ge F_0+1\qquad(h\le|U|<|S|,\ U\subsetneq S).$$

The graph $K$ still has no nonempty $q$-regular subgraph. Only original edges are used; $K$ need not be induced in the host.

**Proof.** The cutoff implies $q\ge32h$. For any bipartite graph on $t$ vertices, simplicity gives at most $t^2/4$ edges. Put

$$\Phi(t)=(q-1)t-t^2/4.$$

This is concave on $[h,2q]$, and its endpoint values satisfy

$$\Phi(h)-F_0-1=\frac{63}{4}h^2-h-1>0,$$

$$\Phi(2q)-F_0-1=q(q-h-2)+16h^2-1>0.$$

Consequently every vertex set $U$ with $h\le|U|\le2q$ has deficit at least $F_0+1$. This estimate applies before or after edges are removed.

Choose a vertex set $S$ of minimum order among those with $|S|\ge2q$ and deficit in the original induced restriction at most $F_0$. Such a set exists because the whole host qualifies. Retain exactly $(q-1)|S|-F_0$ edges on $S$, obtaining $K$. This count is nonnegative: since $|S|\ge2q$,

$$(q-1)|S|-F_0\ge q(2q-2-h)+16h^2>0.$$

It is no larger than the original edge count by the choice of $S$. Thus thinning is possible and gives exact deficit $F_0$.

If $U\subsetneq S$ has $|U|\ge2q$, minimality makes its original deficit greater than $F_0$, hence at least $F_0+1$ by integrality. Removing edges only increases deficit. The small-support estimate supplies the same floor for $h\le|U|<2q$. The estimate at $|S|=2q$ would contradict the exact deficit, so $|S|>2q$.

If $v\in S$ has $d_K(v)\le q-1$, then

$$(q-1)|S\setminus\{v\}|-e(K-v)=F_0+d_K(v)-(q-1)\le F_0.$$

The remaining order is at least $2q$, contradicting the proper-support floor. Hence $d_K(v)\ge q$ for every $v$. The cap and absence of a $q$-regular subgraph are inherited from the host. This proves the lemma. The argument is the ordinary translation of `R882/Normalization.lean:critical_normalization` and `original_graph_normalization`.

**Lemma (controlled peeling).** Let $K$ have shores $A_0,B_0$ and the properties of the preceding lemma. Successively delete a vertex of current degree at most $q+2h-5$ until no such vertex remains. The process leaves a terminal induced restriction $K[T]$ with

$$|T|\ge2q,\qquad q+2h-4\le d_{K[T]}(v)\le2q-2,$$

$$\bigl||A_0\cap T|-|B_0\cap T|\bigr|\le h-1,\qquad
F=(q-1)|T|-e(K[T])=F_0+C,$$

$$0\le C\le4h^2-10h+4,$$

and at most $2h-1$ deleted vertices. Its proper subsets retain the floor

$$(q-1)|U|-e(K[U])\ge F_0+1=F+1-C\qquad(h\le|U|<|T|).$$

**Proof of controlled peeling.** Put $r=q-1$ and let $\delta(U)=r|U|-e(K[U])$. Deleting a vertex of current degree $d$ changes the deficit by $d-r$. Thus, after $s$ deletions, the retained deficit is at most

$$F_0+s(2h-4).$$

Suppose a process makes at least $2h$ deletions, and examine its first $2h$ deletions. Let $P$ be these deleted vertices and $M=S\setminus P$ the retained vertices at that point. The cap gives

$$2e(K[M])+e_K(P,M)\le2r|M|,$$

so $e_K(P,M)\le2\delta(M)$. The partition identity therefore yields

$$\delta(P)=F_0-\delta(M)+e_K(P,M)\le F_0+\delta(M)
\le2F_0+2h(2h-4).$$

Since $|P|=2h$ and $K[P]$ is simple bipartite, $e(K[P])\le h^2$, and hence

$$\delta(P)\ge2h(q-1)-h^2.$$

Subtracting the preceding upper bound from this lower bound gives $27h^2+6h>0$, a contradiction. Every such process therefore deletes at most $2h-1$ vertices. Finiteness permits continuing until no eligible vertex remains.

The terminal set $T$ is nonempty, since $|S|>2q$ and $q\ge32h$. Its minimum degree is at least $q+2h-4$. Each shore must have at least that many vertices, giving $|T|\ge2(q+2h-4)\ge2q$. The cap is inherited. Put $C=\delta(T)-F_0$. If no vertex was removed, $C=0$. Otherwise $T$ is a proper eligible subset of $S$, so the strict floor gives $C\ge1$. The deletion ledger and the count just proved give

$$0\le C\le(2h-1)(2h-4)=4h^2-10h+4.$$

For terminal shore sizes $a,b$, the cap gives $\delta(T)\ge(q-1)|a-b|$. On the other hand,

$$\delta(T)\le hq-12h^2-10h+4<h(q-1).$$

Thus $|a-b|<h$, and integrality gives $|a-b|\le h-1$. Finally every eligible proper subset of $T$ is also an eligible proper subset of $S$, so its inherited floor remains $F_0+1=F+1-C$. This proves all the stated peeling properties. The argument follows the first-prefix contradiction, threshold reduction and imbalance calculation in `R837/Budget.lean`; it uses actual current degrees, including possibly negative individual deletion charges.

These arguments expand normalization and controlled peeling. The unequal-shore selector and the balanced simultaneous cut-payment argument remain unexpanded.

## 3. Why cut geometry needs the reduction

In the balanced branch, the potential floor, degree-shortfall identities and simple bipartite edge bounds constrain negative cuts and low-slack cuts. The proof bounds the symmetric difference of incomparable low-slack witnesses inside one fixed negative anchor by h − 1. It does not assert this bound for arbitrary comparable cuts or arbitrary raw hosts.

A raw-host example shows why that distinction matters. Put d = 2q − 2 and take two disjoint copies of K_(d,d−1) and two of K_(d−1,d), with the first coordinate always on the A shore. The whole graph is balanced, has n = 16q − 20 and deficit 4q − 4, and meets both final budgets at q = 2^18, h = 6. Two left-heavy components are disjoint negative cuts with g = −q; they have no common vertex and their symmetric difference has 8q − 10 vertices. Each component nevertheless contains K_(q,q). Thus this is a counterexample to the shortcut that the raw assumptions already provide the internal cut geometry, not to the theorem.

Common pins and large pools of possible omissions are derived only after the critical-support reduction. The key source modules are `R898/Balanced/CommonPins.lean` and `R898/Balanced/LowSlack.lean`. Their pair identities and potential-floor calculations are substantial parts of the proof, not assumptions one can omit from a standalone account.

## 4. A conditional union estimate, with its proof

Here is a readable part of the quantitative selection argument. Let h ≥ 6 and q > h² − 5. Suppose a finite nonempty family of sets P_i has:

1. 0 ≤ g(P_i) ≤ 3h − 5 and k(P_i) ≤ h − 1.
2. Every incomparable pair has symmetric difference at most h − 1.
3. Every x∈A∩P_i has at most q neighbors in B\P_i.
4. Every y∈B∩P_i has at least q + 1 neighbors in A∩P_i.

These are conditional premises. In the actual proof the P_i are inclusion-minimal hazardous witnesses inside a common negative anchor. The preceding geometry supplies their diameter; minimality supplies the two marginal degree bounds.

Choose P0 of largest cardinality and put Z = B∩(∪_i P_i\P0). Each y∈Z belongs to an incomparable P_i; the nonempty reverse difference gives |P_i\P0| ≤ h − 2. Simplicity and premise 4 give at least q − h + 3 neighbors of y in A∩P0. Hence

$$
(q-h+3)|Z|\le e(A\cap P0,Z)\le q(h-1)+3h-5.
$$

If |Z| ≥ h, the left lower bound exceeds the right upper bound by q − h² + 5 > 0. Thus |Z| ≤ h − 1. Put U = ∪_i P_i and X = A∩(U\P0). Premise 3 gives e(X,B\U) ≤ q|X|. Exact edge accounting yields

$$
g(U)=g(P0)+e(X,B\setminus U)-q|X|+q|Z|-e(A\cap P0,Z).
$$

Consequently

$$
g(U)\le3h-5+(h-3)|Z|\le h^2-h-2.
$$

This bounds the cut value of U, not |U|. It does not by itself imply g(U) ≥ 0. If all P_i avoid a fixed pin a and every set avoiding a has nonnegative g, cut submodularity also yields the separate estimate g(U) ≤ Σ_i g(P_i). The two upper bounds serve different terms in the cut-payment argument and both must be retained.

The isolated counting calculation only needs q > h² − 5. It does not lower the endpoint cutoff, since the preceding reduction, geometry and final selection need their own reserves. The steps that require q ≥ 4096(h + 1)² and produce the 16h² term are not identified in this companion. Minimal failing cuts and local degree inequalities already appear in Enomoto, Ota and Kano's Lemma 4; neither minimality nor submodularity alone is a new method claim. The unresolved comparison is the full chain supplying and combining these premises. Source: `R898/Balanced/MinimalUnion.lean`.

## 6. Deriving the three-halves bound

Let x = √q and h = floor(x/64) − 1. For q ≥ 2^18, x ≥ 512, so h ≥ 6 and 4096(h + 1)² ≤ q. Also h ≥ x/64 − 2 and 16h² ≤ q/256, giving

$$
hq-16h^2\ge(x/64-2)q-q/256\ge xq/128=q^{3/2}/128.
$$

For the second inequality it suffices that x ≥ 513/2, which follows from x ≥ 512. Apply the parameterized theorem. The formal arithmetic uses the natural floor and literal `Real.rpow`; see `R898/Quantitative.lean`. This is a corollary of the same theorem chain, not a separate new application.

### Controlled peeling and shore imbalance

Let h ≥ 6, q ≥ 4096(h + 1)² and F0 = hq − 16h². Suppose K is a finite simple bipartite graph on disjoint shores A0,B0, with n0 = |A0| + |B0| ≥ 2q, every degree between q and 2q − 2, total deficit F0, and

$$
(q-1)|U|-e(K[U])\ge F0+1
\quad\text{whenever }h\le |U|<n0.
$$

Sequentially delete vertices whose current degree is at most q + 2h − 5 until the remaining graph has minimum degree at least q + 2h − 4. The formal peeling lemma guarantees a terminal induced support T with:

- |T| ≥ 2q and q + 2h − 4 ≤ d_(K[T])(v) ≤ 2q − 2 for every v∈T;
- at most 2h − 1 deleted vertices;
- deficit F = F0 + C with 0 ≤ C ≤ 4h² − 10h + 4;
- absolute difference between the two retained shore sizes at most h − 1;
- (q − 1)|U| − e(K[U]) ≥ F + 1 − C for h ≤ |U| < |T| and U⊆T.

Each deletion changes the deficit by its current degree minus q − 1. Its upper contribution is therefore 2h − 4; the deletion-count bound gives (2h − 1)(2h − 4) = 4h² − 10h + 4. Section 2 supplies the deletion-count, survival and imbalance arguments. Their formal counterparts in `R837/Budget.lean` are `deletion_count`, `threshold_reduction`, `exists_threshold_reduction` and `retained_imbalance`. The original proper-support floor gives nonnegative total C and survives restriction; the exact floor in the conclusion is F + 1 − C.

### Unequal-shore omission selector

Let h,q satisfy the same cutoff, let C be an integer with 0 ≤ C ≤ 4h² − 10h + 4, and let G be a finite simple bipartite graph whose disjoint shores A,B cover V. Suppose

$$
|V|\ge2q,\quad |B|=|A|+j,\quad1\le j\le h-1,
$$

$$
q\le d(v)\le2q-2\quad(v\in V),\qquad
 d(a)\ge q+2h-4\quad(a\in A),
$$

$$
f(V)=F0+C,\qquad f(U)\ge F0+1
\quad\text{for }h\le|U|<|V|,
$$

where f(U) = (q − 1)|U| − e(G[U]). Then there is an actual set O⊆B with |O| = j and an original subgraph H whose vertex set is exactly A∪(B\O), is nonempty, and has degree q at every one of its vertices.

This is the literal interface `Erdos585.R882.Positive.error_floor_positive_selector` in `R882/Positive/Main.lean`. It also returns the cut inequalities used to construct H, without requiring a successful omission from the caller. Its proof is not reproduced here. This interface is not in the registry; the registered `Erdos585.R863.error_floor_positive_selector` is an earlier version with cutoff 2^20(h + e + 1)² ≤ q. Its module, `R882/Positive/Main.lean`, is built and kernel-replayed in the v0.4 transcript; no `./check.sh` row for this declaration is recorded there. The specific stronger minimum degree is needed only on the smaller shore in this interface; the preceding peeling supplies it on both. ### Mixed-insertion coverage in the balanced branch

Here the intermediate graph has balanced disjoint shores A,B covering V, order at least 2q and q ≤ d(v) ≤ 2q − 2. Retain the same h,q,C and put F = F0 + C. The coverage lemmas use

$$
f(V)=F,\quad2q\le F\le hq-12h^2,\qquad
f(U)\ge F+1-C\quad(h\le|U|<|V|).
$$

Let M be the greatest global minimizer of g with g(M)<0. Thus g(M) ≤ g(W) for every W⊆V, and every W with g(W)=g(M) is contained in M. By the common-pin and omission-pool lemmas (`R898/Balanced/CommonPins.lean`, not proved here), there exist a∈A∩M that belongs to every negative g-cut and b∈B\M with the two lightness bounds

$$
d_{B\setminus M}(a)\le3h-4,\qquad
 d_{A\cap M}(b)\le3h-4.
$$

If W⊆V has a∉W, b∈W, |W\M|<h and g(W)≤q−1, put P=W∩M and E=W\M. The coverage argument first yields

$$
0\le g(P)\le4h-7,\quad |M\setminus P|\ge h,\quad
 e(P,V\setminus M)\le6h-10+C.
$$

The middle inequality is essential: it makes the omitted anchor part eligible for the original floor. The mixed-insertion payment has this separate exact interface: for any P⊆M and T⊆V\M with |T|≤h−2 and e(P,V\M)≤6h−10+C, and any b∈B,

$$
d_T(b)\le g(P\cup T)-g(P).
$$

The hypotheses on the balanced intermediate graph and greatest minimizer remain in force. Maximality supplies a positive insertion cost on the A part of T; submodularity transports it from M to P. The actual edges from the B part of T provide the remaining edge tax. The full proof is in `retained_insertion_payment`; its boundary and floor prerequisites cannot be dropped.

For T=E\{b}, the exact insertion identity is

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(P\cup E)={}&g(P)+q-d_P(b)\\
 &+g(M\cup T)-g(M)+e(M\setminus P,T)-d_T(b).
\end{aligned}
$$

The mixed payment and disjoint-set cut accounting make the last line nonnegative. Since P∪E=W and g(W)≤q−1, this gives g(P)+1≤d_P(b). Thus P⊆M avoids a and is a hazard witness for b. A pair selected to avoid hazards rules out such W. Band localization and the swapped orientation cover the other tiny-difference alternative, completing the universal omission-cut inequalities.

The exact formal sources are `original_floor_tiny_boundary`, `left_insertion_retained`, `retained_insertion_payment`, `insertion_identity` and `tiny_killer_hazard` in `R898/Balanced/Coverage.lean`. The floor-eligibility and mixed edge-tax derivations remain unexpanded prose obligations, alongside the unequal-shore selector. These are formal-source dependencies, rather than assumptions of the final theorem. Controlled peeling is proved in section 2.

## Retained deficit 4q − 44 theorem: partial proof roadmap

Let q ≥ 25600 be an integer. If G is a finite simple bipartite graph with n ≥ 2q, maximum degree at most 2q − 2, and e(G) ≥ (q − 1)n − (4q − 44), then G contains a nonempty q-regular subgraph on freely chosen original vertices and edges.

For the common parameter range q ≥ 25600 and the same n, this sufficient threshold is q − 12 edges below the H49 threshold (3q − 32) and 2q − 39 below the earlier L34 threshold (2q − 5). H49 remains useful for q ≥ 16384, and L34 for q ≥ 5 with its separate support guarantee. For prime-power q ≥ 25600, the H4 threshold (4q − 44) is 3q − 42 below the integer AFK Remark 3.6 trigger (q − 1)n − q + 2. This compares only the named statements, not all extraction methods or historical novelty. The H4 conclusion does not inherit the old 4q − 6 support bound.

Formal endpoint: `R788/Endpoint.lean:Erdos585.H4BareR788.bare_h4_original_caller`.
The following is a source-faithful roadmap, not a full standalone written proof. The imported
unequal-shore, core-construction, factor-cut and hazard-selection proofs remain to be expanded.

1. Strong induction on order and thinning of actual original edges reduce to exact density
   with deficit 4q − 44. The bipartite rectangle bound rules out order 2q. Deleting a vertex of
   degree at most q − 1 then contradicts induction, deriving minimum degree q internally.
2. Induction supplies defect at least 4q − 43 on every proper support of order at
   least 2q. The small-support estimate supplies the same floor at orders 4 through 2q − 1.
   These are floors on all supports, not only balanced ones.
3. Sorted shores have imbalance at most three by the cap and exact density. The three
   unequal cases use the corresponding original-graph repair theorems at h = 4, offset 44.
4. The balanced case constructs a proper balanced original induced core of order at least
   2q, induced minimum degree q, cap 2q − 2 and no regular subgraph. Its defect is exactly
   C + 1 or C + 2, namely 4q − 43 or 4q − 42. Internal proper-support floors from order four
   belong to this core record and must be distinguished from the global critical floor.
5. The internally supplied balanced-core factor theorem treats both slack branches. With
   nonnegative slack, bipartite factor cuts give a spanning core factor. With negative slack,
   common pins and global minimum-slack cuts, hazard bounds and large pools select one
   omission pair avoiding both hazard families. In this retained R788 proof, tiny insertion control is restricted to
   h = 3 or 4; the arbitrary-h argument for the leading theorem is the one in sections 2 to 8. Unit cuts give a factor
   after omitting one vertex from each core shore, using only original edges.
6. Either factor contradicts the inherited absence of a regular subgraph. Transport through
   the induced core and thinned graph returns the required original subgraph, with no
   remaining caller-supplied slack, core or factor premise.

Feasibility: K_(2q−2,2q−2) has n = 4q − 4, maximum degree 2q − 2 and
4(q − 1)² edges, exceeding the H4 threshold by 4q − 44. It contains K_(q,q).
This shows nonvacuity, not sharpness. The source review inspected the endpoint assembly
and selected interfaces; it did not re-audit every transitive proof or establish novelty.

## Large-degree proof roadmap and remaining exposition obligations

The source endpoint is: if q is a natural number at least 16384, G is finite, simple and bipartite, n≥2q, Δ(G)≤2q−2, and e(G)≥(q−1)n−(3q−32), then G contains a nonempty q-regular subgraph. Its support is freely chosen, and the subgraph need not be induced or connected.

This is a proof spine, not a complete paper proof. The imported equality-support, unequal-shore, negative-cut rigidity, and pair-selection lemmas below remain unexpanded here.

Labels in this spine: R706, R701, R699, R703, R704, R707 and R715 are the source folders of the Lean modules that carry each step (navigation aids, not theorem names); Host 3 and offset(3) are the h = 3 instance of the host structure and its offset constant 32; construct_q0 and Noq are the names of the support-construction lemma and of the no-regular-subgraph hypothesis in those modules.

1. **Minimal counterexample and thinning (R706).** Strong induction is on vertex order, over arbitrary finite vertex types. Thin edges to exact equality e=(q−1)n−(3q−32), retaining bipartiteness, the cap, and absence of an original q-regular subgraph. The n=2q boundary is impossible: bipartite simplicity gives e≤q², but the required e is 2q²−5q+32>q² for q≥16384. Thus deleting a vertex leaves enough vertices for induction.
2. **Critical inputs are derived (R706).** Every proper induced support of order at least 2q has deficit (q−1)|S|−e(G[S])≥3q−31, else induction gives a regular subgraph lifting to G. A vertex of degree at most q−1 could be deleted while preserving the dense threshold, so minimum degree is at least q. ## Feasibility and support, with elementary proofs

The hypotheses are nonvacuous for every allowed q: take K_(2q−2,2q−2). Its order is 4q−4≥2q, cap is 2q−2, and e=(2q−2)²=(q−1)(4q−4), exceeding the threshold by 3q−32. This verifies feasibility, not optimality or novelty. At n=2q the hypotheses are impossible, as above; n≥2q is a convenient theorem guard, not a claim of feasible examples at every guarded order.

Every nonempty positive-q regular simple bipartite graph has at least 2q vertices. Pick a vertex; it has q distinct neighbors on the opposite shore. Any such neighbor has q distinct neighbors on the first shore. Hence both shores have at least q vertices. Also q|L|=e=q|R|, so the support shores are balanced. These are generic consequences, not a new H49 large-support result. This review has no proof of a larger bound depending on original n, and H49's literal conclusion states none.

## Earlier theorem: detailed argument and retained dependencies

# Dense regular-subgraph theorem: q ≥ 6 bipartite proof, q = 5 and even-case dependencies

**Reading guide.** This explanation was written by AI agents from the project's Lean sources. It is not a novelty claim or an independent proof check. The detailed bipartite argument below covers integers q ≥ 6, using the integral max-flow theorem. The stated q = 5 endpoint and the general-graph extension have explicit exposition dependencies at the end. Consequently this document is not yet a self-contained proof of the entire stated package.

## Statements and terminology

All graphs are finite and simple. Write n = |V(G)| and e(G) = |E(G)|. A q-regular subgraph H consists of selected original vertices and selected original edges, with degree q at each selected vertex. It need not be induced or spanning. Its support is V(H).

**Bipartite theorem.** Let q ≥ 5 be an integer. If G is bipartite and

$$
n\ge2q,\qquad \Delta(G)\le2q-2,\qquad
 e(G)\ge(q-1)n-(2q-5),
$$

then there exists a nonempty q-regular subgraph H. Moreover, some such H satisfies |V(H)| ≥ 4q−6.

**Even-degree theorem.** Let q ≥ 6 be even. If G is a simple graph, n ≥ 2,

$$
\Delta(G)\le2q-2,\qquad e(G)\ge(q-1)n-(q-2),
$$

then some nonempty q-regular subgraph H satisfies |V(H)| ≥ 2q−1.

These are existential support bounds. They do not assert that every regular subgraph is large. Neither conclusion asserts spanningness, a prescribed support, a cycle pair, optimality, or novelty.

## The critical bipartite lemma

Fix q ≥ 6, put r = q−1, D = 2q−2, c = 2q−5, and let A,B be disjoint shores of a bipartite graph K. Degrees below are in K between the two shores. For X ⊆ A and Y ⊆ B define

$$
f(X,Y)=r(|X|+|Y|)-e_K(X,Y).
$$

**Critical lemma.** Suppose |A|+|B| ≥ 2q, every vertex has degree between q and D,

$$
e_K(A,B)=r(|A|+|B|)-c,
$$

and f(X,Y) ≥ c+1 for every X ⊆ A, Y ⊆ B with
3 ≤ |X|+|Y| < |A|+|B|. Then K contains a nonempty q-regular subgraph. More precisely, after interchanging shores if necessary, either |B|=|A| and K has a spanning q-factor, or |B|=|A|+1 and deleting one vertex of B leaves a graph with a spanning q-factor.

Here a spanning q-factor selects edges so that every vertex of the indicated graph has degree q. The critical lemma's floor is required only for orders at least three, and only for proper subsets. Its equivalent Lean formulation uses the potential 2f and the floor 4q−8.

### Complementary tests

Write |B|=|A|+j with j ≥ 0. Let δ(a)=D−d(a) and α=Σ_{a∈A}δ(a). The exact edge count gives

$$
\alpha=c-rj\ge0.
$$

Since c<2r, j is 0 or 1. For T ⊆ B put

$$
\phi_a(T)=\max(0,d_T(a)-d(a)+q),\qquad
\Theta(T)=qj-q|T|+\sum_{a\in A}\phi_a(T),
$$

and C(T)={a∈A : d_T(a)>d(a)−q}. Then Θ(B)=0 and Θ(∅)=qj. The exception at the empty set matters when j=1.

For nonempty T with |T|≤q−2, simplicity gives φ_a(T)≤δ(a), hence Θ(T)≤c+j−q|T|. This is negative for 2≤|T|≤q−2. For a singleton, φ_a is 1 only at degree-q neighbors. Each such row consumes q−2 units of α. When j=0, α=2q−5 allows at most one such row; when j=1, α=q−4 allows none. Thus every **nonempty positive test** has |T|≥q−1 and T≠B.

For a positive test set C=C(T), k=|C|−|T| and α_C=Σ_{a∈C}δ(a). Direct expansion gives

$$
\Theta(T)=qj+k+\alpha_C-f(C,T).
$$

Also Θ(T)≤q(j+k), so positivity implies k≥1−j. Thus |C|+|T|≥3, and T≠B makes C∪T proper. The critical floor applies. Since α_C≤c−rj, it yields

$$
\Theta(T)\le j+k-1.
$$

The column cap gives e(C,T)≤D|T|, so f(C,T)≥rk. Consequently

$$
\Theta(T)\le c+j-(q-2)k.
$$

If k≥2 the right side is nonpositive. Hence k≤1. Combining these integer inequalities shows that every nonempty positive test necessarily satisfies

$$
j=1,\quad k=1,\quad \Theta(T)=1,\quad
\alpha_C=q-4,\quad f(C,T)=2q-4.
$$

This is the exact positive-test classification. It is not a classification of every arbitrary failed cut. In particular, every deficient row a, meaning δ(a)>0, belongs to C(T) for every nonempty positive test: the canonical set captures the entire deficiency α=q−4.

### A common omitted vertex

In the balanced case j=0, the classification rules out all nonempty positive tests, and Θ(∅)=0. Thus Θ(T)≤0 for every T.

For j=1, choose a deficient row a, which exists because α=q−4>0. For any positive T, remove a from C(T). The remaining set together with T is proper and has order at least three: |T|≥q−1 and |C(T)|=|T|+1. The floor and the exact value of f(C(T),T) give

$$
f(C(T)\setminus\{a\},T)
 =f(C(T),T)-r+d_T(a)\ge f(C(T),T),
$$

so d_T(a)≥r.

The function Θ is supermodular. Indeed, each positive part max(0,t−d(a)+q) has nondecreasing successive differences; applying that observation to the neighbor counts in an intersection and the two differences proves the supermodular inequality after summation.

Choose an inclusion-maximal nonempty positive set M, if one exists. Every other nonempty positive set T is contained in M. Otherwise M∪T strictly contains M and has Θ≤0. If M∩T were nonempty, its value would be at most 1, whereas supermodularity would give 2≤1. Thus M,T would be disjoint. But the same deficient row a has at least r neighbors in each, forcing d(a)≥2r=D, contrary to its deficiency d(a)<D.

Since M is proper, choose p∈B\M. Every T containing p has Θ(T)≤0. If there are no nonempty positive tests, any p∈B works. This proves one simultaneous omission, rather than different omissions for different tests.

### Recovering original edges by integral flow

For X⊆B and T=B\X, direct degree expansion gives

$$
\sum_{a\in A}\min(q,d_X(a))-q|X|=-\Theta(T).
$$

In the balanced case this is nonnegative for every X⊆B. In the surplus case restrict X to B'=B\{p}; then p∈T makes it nonnegative as well, and |B'|=|A|.

Use a network with source-to-B' capacities q, unit arcs for the original edges from B' to A, and A-to-sink capacities q. For fixed source-side X⊆B', minimizing over the A choices gives cut capacity
q(|B'|−|X|)+Σ_a min(q,d_X(a)), at least q|B'|. Integral max-flow therefore selects original edges saturating every vertex of A and B'. Both shores are nonempty, since their original minimum degree was q. This proves the critical lemma.

## From density to criticality, and the support bound

For q≥6, choose shores P,Q of minimum total order among restrictions of G with order at least 2q and e(P,Q)≥r(|P|+|Q|)−c. Thin edges to equality. The bipartite product ceiling excludes order 2q: at that order equality requires 2q²−4q+5 edges, exceeding q².

Deleting a vertex of degree at most r would leave a smaller dense restriction of order at least 2q, contradicting minimality. Hence the thinned graph has minimum degree q. Every proper restriction of order at least 2q has f≥c+1 by minimality and integrality. For orders 3 through 2q−1, the product ceiling e(X,Y)≤(|X|+|Y|)²/4 supplies the same floor: the concave expression rm−m²/4 is minimized at an endpoint of this interval, and both endpoint values exceed 2q−4 for q≥6. Thus the critical lemma applies.

There is a stronger order estimate for any dense bipartite source of order m≥2q. If m≤4q−6, then

$$
4(rm-c)-4|P||Q|
 =8+(m-2)(4q-m-6)+(|P|-|Q|)^2>0,
$$

contradicting e(P,Q)≤|P||Q|. Therefore m≥4q−5. The critical construction retains all source vertices or omits only one, giving support at least 4q−6. Thinning and restriction preserve original vertex labels and original edges, so the selected subgraph transports directly to G.

## General graphs with even q: inspected proof sketch

Choose a minimum dense vertex set U of order at least two, and thin its induced graph to e(K)=r|U|−(q−2). Every nonempty proper X⊂U then has D|X|−2e_K(X)≥D; singletons use simplicity, and larger X use minimality and integrality. The simple-graph ceiling already forces |U|≥2q−1.

If K has a spanning q-factor, this proves the stated support bound. Otherwise the even-factor criterion supplies a negative barrier. In the inspected barrier calculation, component deficiency charges and parity residuals force its two disjoint sets S,T to cover U with |S|=|T|+1. They also give

$$
2e_K(S)\le q-2,\qquad
2\sum_{v\in T}(D-d_K(v))+2e_K(T)\le q-4.
$$

Thus e_K(S)+e_K(T)≤q−3, and the crossing graph has at least r|U|−(2q−5) edges. The same calculation yields |S|≥q+2, hence |U|≥2q. Apply the bipartite theorem to crossing edges. Any nonempty simple bipartite q-regular subgraph has at least q vertices on each shore, giving support at least 2q in this branch. Injective inclusion transports the result to G.
