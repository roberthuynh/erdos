# v0.4: degree-capped regular subgraphs

This note records selected additions to the earlier release. All graphs here are finite and
simple. Write n = |V(G)| and e = |E(G)|. A q-regular subgraph means a nonempty subgraph on a
chosen vertex set S, with every vertex of S having degree q in that subgraph. It need not be
induced, spanning, connected or unique. A q-factor of a specified graph instead spans that graph.

The separate [v0.4.1 corollary](V0.4.1.md) extends the convenient real-deficit endpoint to q ≥ 200704. The v0.4 statements below and their original endpoints are retained.

## 1. Statements and formal evidence

**Leading v0.4 theorem.** Let q ≥ 2^18 = 262144 be an integer. Every finite simple bipartite graph G on n ≥ 2q vertices, with maximum degree at most 2q − 2 and

$$
e(G)\ge(q-1)n-q^{3/2}/128,
$$

contains a nonempty q-regular subgraph using original vertices and edges. The power is interpreted over the reals; the equivalent integer allowance is floor(q^(3/2)/128).

**Parameterized theorem.** For integers h ≥ 6 and q ≥ 4096(h + 1)², the same conclusion holds with the same order and degree-cap assumptions when

$$
D=(q-1)n-e(G)\le hq-16h^2.
$$

The deficit D is signed integer subtraction in the parameterized statement and signed real subtraction in the fractional-power statement. The degree cap makes it nonnegative. The total vertex-degree shortfall from 2q − 2 is 2D, not D. Neither theorem asks the caller for minimum degree, balanced shores, a critical core or successful cuts. Neither promises spanning, induced or connected support, or transfers the earlier 4q − 6 support guarantee.

Formal endpoints: `R898/Main.lean:Erdos585.R898.superlinear_forcing` and `R898/Main.lean:Erdos585.R898.every_h_forcing`.

Labels used in this note: H49 is the 3q − 32 theorem (q ≥ 16384), H4 the 4q − 44 theorem (q ≥ 25600), H5 the 5q − 58 theorem (q ≥ 36864) and L34 the 2q − 5 theorem (q ≥ 5). Identifiers such as R898 name source folders, not theorems.

At q = 2^18, the real allowance is 4q; the parameterized statement with h = 7 allows 7q − 784. The real formula is implied by the parameterized theorem at every q ≥ 2^18 (take h = floor(√q/64) − 1, so the allowance is about q^(3/2)/64 for large q); for 2^18 ≤ q ≤ 409576 it is also weaker than the retained D ≤ 5q − 58 theorem.

**Retained smaller-cutoff theorem.** For q ≥ 36864, the same bipartite order and cap assumptions suffice when D ≤ 5q − 58. Its endpoint is `R831/Endpoint.lean:Erdos585.H5BareR831.bare_h5_original_caller`.

**Retained deficit 4q − 44 theorem.** Let q ≥ 25600 be an integer. If G is a finite simple bipartite graph with n ≥ 2q, maximum degree at most 2q − 2, and e(G) ≥ (q − 1)n − (4q − 44), then G contains a nonempty q-regular subgraph on freely chosen original vertices and edges.

For the common parameter range q ≥ 25600 and the same n, this sufficient threshold is q − 12 edges below the H49 threshold (3q − 32) and 2q − 39 below the earlier L34 threshold (2q − 5). H49 remains useful for q ≥ 16384, and L34 for q ≥ 5 with its separate support guarantee. For prime-power q ≥ 25600, the H4 threshold (4q − 44) is 3q − 42 below the integer AFK Remark 3.6 trigger (q − 1)n − q + 2. This compares only the named statements, not all extraction methods or historical novelty. The H4 conclusion does not inherit the old 4q − 6 support bound.

**Earlier large-degree bipartite theorem.** Let q ≥ 16384 be an integer. If G is a finite simple bipartite graph with n ≥ 2q, maximum degree at most 2q − 2, and e(G) ≥ (q − 1)n − (3q − 32), then G contains a nonempty q-regular subgraph on a freely chosen support of original vertices and edges.

This lowers the earlier bipartite existence threshold by q − 27 edges at the same q,n,
but does not transfer the earlier 4q − 6 support conclusion.

**Earlier bipartite theorem.** Let q ≥ 5 be an integer. If G is bipartite, n ≥ 2q,
Δ(G) ≤ 2q − 2 and e ≥ (q − 1)n − (2q − 5), then there exists a q-regular subgraph
whose vertex set has size at least 4q − 6.

**Even-degree theorem.** Let q ≥ 6 be even. If n ≥ 2, Δ(G) ≤ 2q − 2 and
e ≥ (q − 1)n − (q − 2), then there exists a q-regular subgraph whose vertex set has
size at least 2q − 1. The host need not be bipartite.

**Quartic theorem.** If G is bipartite, n ≥ 4, Δ(G) ≤ 6 and e ≥ 3n − 6, then G
contains a nonempty 4-regular subgraph.

The support bounds assert the existence of some sufficiently large subgraph. They do not put
those lower bounds on every regular subgraph of the host. In particular, the theorem does not
say that all n vertices can be used. Here and in Lean, a subgraph's degree is measured on its
own vertex set. The hypotheses make the displayed ordinary integer inequalities equivalent to
the Lean statements, including the natural-number subtraction in the bipartite statement.

### Parameterized obstruction and exponent gap

For every integer q ≥ 5 and every n ≥ 4q − 5, the circular construction is bipartite, has maximum degree at most 2q − 2 and exactly (q − 1)n − (q² − 3q + 4) edges, and avoids every nonempty q-regular subgraph. Its endpoint is `Openmath/Proofs/PaperSupport/BqCircularLowerDirect.lean:BqCircularLowerFamily.all_orders`. It is registered in `scripts/headline-theorems.json` and checked by `scripts/check_axioms.py` and `./check.sh`.

Let F*(q) be the largest integer allowance forcing a nonempty q-regular subgraph in every simple bipartite host of order n ≥ 2q and maximum degree at most 2q − 2. For q ≥ 2^18, these results give

$$
\max\{hq-16h^2 : h\ge 6,\ 4096(h+1)^2\le q\}\le F^*(q)\le q^2-3q+3,
$$

and the left side exceeds both floor(q^{3/2}/128) and q^{3/2}/64 − (513/256)q. The upper bound follows from an avoiding example at the next integer deficit. The parameterized GeneralStar family gives avoiders with smaller deficit at particular q, so this displayed upper bound is not the best this repository proves; the circular bound is shown because it holds for every q ≥ 5. The two exponents differ; this does not determine the optimal order or constants. For 200704 ≤ q < 2^18 the parameterized theorem with h = 6 gives F*(q) ≥ 6q − 576; below q = 200704 the retained rows (5q − 58, 4q − 44, 3q − 32 and 2q − 5) give the known lower bounds on F*(q).

## 3. Relation to Erdős 585 and prior work

Two edge-disjoint cycles on the same vertex set form a 4-regular subgraph. To go back from a
4-regular subgraph to a pair requires finding a suitable decomposition into two cycles on the
same support. None of the theorems above supplies that step. No upper bound for the original
extremal function f(n) beyond the published bounds is claimed.

A standard argument from König's theorem, that every regular bipartite multigraph decomposes into perfect matchings, gives an elementary all-integer comparator at D ≤ q − 2. If the host shores have sizes a,b, the cap implies D ≥ (q − 1)|a − b|. Thus this allowance forces equal shores. Add D auxiliary edges to complete the graph to a (2q − 2)-regular bipartite multigraph on those shores. Its perfect-matching decomposition has at most D matchings using added edges, leaving at least (2q − 2) − D ≥ q clean perfect matchings. Their union is a spanning q-factor using original edges. The added parallel edges occur only in the auxiliary completion. This proof does not extend automatically to the larger allowance: the host can then have isolated vertices or unequal components.

For prime-power q, Alon, Friedland and Kalai's Remark 3.6 gives a nonempty q-divisible subgraph of a bipartite graph when e > (q − 1)(n − 1). Under the cap 2q − 2 every positive q-divisible degree is q. This yields the same integer allowance q − 2 via a different route. [AFK, printed p. 85](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/Publications/Regular%20subgraphs%20of%20almost%20regular%20graphs.pdf).

At q = 2^18 the three-halves allowance is 4q, and as q grows its ratio to q − 2 grows without bound. At D = q − 1 the host need not have a spanning q-factor: K_{2q−2,2q−2} plus an isolated vertex has D = q − 1, no spanning q-factor, and a nonempty q-regular subgraph. An argument that produces a spanning q-factor of the whole host therefore stops at D ≤ q − 2; arguments on a chosen support, such as the one in this repository, are not limited by it. This comparison did not identify or exclude a published non-spanning bound between q − 2 and q^(3/2)/128. This is a comparison within the stated cap-and-deficit host class. The average degree is at least 2q − 2 − 2D/n, so for n much larger than q the host is extremely close to the cap. No unrestricted density or Erdős 585 improvement follows.

Classical factor criteria explain feasibility on a specified support. Minimal failing cuts and their local degree inequalities also occur in Enomoto, Ota and Kano's published 1988 paper. No specialist has assessed the novelty of the theorem or of its proof mechanism. The open comparison concerns the full mechanism that derives a critical support and selects compatible omissions under the aggregate-deficit hypothesis. Neither a failed guard for one published theorem nor a finite search excludes all classical derivations. [Enomoto–Ota–Kano](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.3190120115).

The [literature comparison](../literature/README.md) records original-source checks, exact hypotheses and limits. It distinguishes Hao, Li and Yu's published 2025 factor theorem from Fan and Lin's separate work, and includes the original Bermond–Las Vergnas statements. No direct published implication of the full endpoint was found in the statements checked. Worldwide novelty and method novelty remain unresolved, and no separately established new application is claimed.
