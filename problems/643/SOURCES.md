# Sources, attribution and comparison

These are specific statement comparisons with named primary sources. They do not certify worldwide novelty, exclude unpublished work or independently validate every cited proof. Papers are cited, not redistributed.

## Problem and conjecture

P. Erdős, *Problems and results in combinatorial analysis*, Congressus Numerantium 19 (1977), 3–12, [primary text](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1977-24.pdf), printed p.11, Section II item 6, discusses distinct unions and an equal-disjoint-union four-triple witness, crediting a generalization to Bollobás and Erdős. This is an early problem source; it is not attributed the precise coefficient-one conjecture below. An exact 1997 passage was not available in the reading supporting this document and is not asserted here.

Z. Füredi, *Hypergraphs in which all disjoint pairs have distinct unions*, Combinatorica 4 (1984), 161–168, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579216), Conjecture 1.4, conjectures the binomial upper bound for fixed r≥3 and sufficiently large n. The verified all-order r=3 inequality is stronger in its order range. Füredi's Theorem 1.2 supplies the star-plus-matching lower bound. Neither construction nor conjecture is new here.

The [problem page](https://www.erdosproblems.com/643) uses a minimum forcing threshold, while the cited extremal papers use a maximum avoiding count. In this presentation f(n;3)=ex₃(n)+1. At the recorded formal-conjectures revision `2d73cf81dec443f1d4aa1eb45129446152cdf708`, the r=3 asymptotic statement is the t=3 specialization of `Erdos643.erdos_643.parts.ii`; that entire declaration quantifies over all t≥3. No r=3 argument alone discharges the full declaration. The separate [actual-import bridge](formal-evidence/upstream-bridge/README.md) uses exact upstream definitions from commit `95b199bfbc08f7b1183196bab59ed3b08f27c2c9` and transfers the 24/23 bound to the extremal and forcing expressions. It does not discharge the coefficient-one asymptotic statement or the full declaration. The separate accepted final coefficient-one proof package does establish the exact triple extremal and forcing asymptotics through `Erdos643R3.CoefficientOneFinal.extremal_ratio_tendsto_one` and `Erdos643R3.CoefficientOneFinal.forcing_isEquivalent`. Its historical 69-module exported-source check is separate from the earlier 24/23 bridge; the current expanded combined package verifies all 126 exact modules. The [combined proof package](coefficient-one-design/README.md) supplies the complete current source and checking instructions. Neither result proves a statement for every uniformity at least three.

## Classical equality construction

Füredi's Example 1.3 fills every block of an S(2,5,n) with its ten triples. Hanani's design existence results give such designs for n≥5 with n≡1 or5 (mod20), yielding binom(n,2) triples. The empty triple family handles n=1 separately, with extremal value zero. In the formal block-design convention the empty one-point block family satisfies pair coverage vacuously; it is not a nontrivial five-block design. Order five uses a single five-point block. The attribution to Hanani concerns the classical nontrivial existence construction. Together with the verified upper theorem, the cited existence theorem gives ex₃(n)=binom(n,2) and f(n;3)=binom(n,2)+1 at those orders. The expanded formal construction now proves general existence at every admissible order, with complete official verification and independent exact-source acceptance. Its exact declarations are `Erdos643Design.HananiExistence.exists_design`, `Erdos643R3.CoefficientOneHanani.extremal_eq` and `Erdos643R3.CoefficientOneFullSolution.forcing_eq`. The combined package rebuilds and independently kernel-replays all 126 modules and checks 23 exact declarations and their allowed axioms. The supplied-design theorem keeps its hypothesis; the separate existence construction supplies it. This is existence, not a classification; equality in the shadow/co-link inequality is a different condition.

The attribution to H. Hanani is supported by the original papers, now obtained and visually read, as well as Füredi's Example 1.3 and Pikhurko–Verstraëte's introduction. All 26 pages of the 1961 paper (printed 361–386) and the complete one-page 1965 note (printed 711) were read from rendered originals. The 1961 statement (7.10), pp.378–380, leaves a possible 141-point exception; the 1965 note supplies that case and explicitly removes the exception. This reading did not independently revalidate every finite table/block entry or formally audit Hanani's historical proof. The package's separate constructive Lean proof supplies its mathematical evidence. Earlier publisher/JSTOR downloads returned HTML challenges; later successful acquisition supersedes that earlier access limit, without erasing the failed attempts:

- H. Hanani, *The existence and construction of balanced incomplete block designs*, Annals of Mathematical Statistics 32 (1961), 361–386, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177705047).
- H. Hanani, *A balanced incomplete block design*, Annals of Mathematical Statistics 36 (1965), 711, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177700182).

## Design-only predecessor and construction reading

Howard's printed page 9 records the classification of simple disjoint-union-free `(n,3,3)` designs by P. J. Dukes and A. C. H. Ling, *Disjoint union-free designs with block size three*, Bulletin of the Institute of Combinatorics and its Applications 45 (2005), 5–10. Its prescribed pair multiplicity differs from a classification of all extremal triple systems. The original six-page article was not obtained; the thesis passage and bibliographic identity were checked, not that article's complete proof.

Smith's author-hosted [23 February 2004 exposition](https://argilo.net/files/bibd.pdf) was read in full, all fourteen pages. It is a construction route map, not Hanani's original proof or a substitute for exact Lean verification. Its proposed composite transversal step and several modulus/deletion details require independent proofs. The classical existence result is not new mathematics here. The expanded general formal construction has its own complete accepted exact-source verification. This formalizes known classical mathematics; it does not establish a new design-existence theorem or a full audit of the historical original proofs.

## Limits

The comparisons concern the indicated statements and versions, not a full proof audit of all sources. Füredi statement/construction context and Pikhurko–Verstraëte statements/concluding discussion were reread; earlier exact primary-page reading for Frankl–Füredi and Mubayi was reused. HMY v1 and its exact higher-uniformity theorem were checked, and Howard printed pages 9 and 28 were reread. This focused access/version pass is not a new exhaustive citation search. The Hanani originals have now been visually read at the scope stated above. The original Dukes–Ling article, the exact 1997 passage and the full citing literature remain unread; many previously screened citers were inspected only through records or reviews. A newer library search or a blank proof-claim page does not establish absence of prior or parallel work. No exhaustive database coverage or first-discovery claim is made. Work on Brown–Erdős–Sós limiting density, minimum codegree or minimum-size maximal systems addresses different questions and is not claimed as an established derivation of this charging inequality.
