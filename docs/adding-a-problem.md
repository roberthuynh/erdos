# Adding a problem

The [collection catalogue](../README.md#collection) lists the current problem packages. Each package states its own evidence and limitations. Do not create empty result directories as placeholders.

A released package lives at `problems/<number>/`. Use the same README section order for every problem: **The problem**, **Main results**, **Status**, **Prior work**, **Proofs and findings**, **AI use**, **Verification**, and **Citation and corrections**. Begin with the question and a short explanation of its terms. Give exact hypotheses and an explicit evidence description beside each result. New or changed theorem claims require full Lean verification of the exact stated conclusion and hypotheses. Computational results and historical written candidates retain their own evidence labels; neither substitutes for a verified theorem. Put the relation to the original problem and review state under Status. A later status paragraph must not be the first indication that an earlier claim is unproved or unformalized. Preserve meaningful result-specific subsections rather than copying irrelevant Lean or census sections into every package. Keep first-person prose on the collection homepage; link to it from a short, problem-specific AI disclosure. Use factual, impersonal language for mathematical summaries and verification records. Do not invent first-person narrative. Place build details under Verification and repository-layout notes in the migration guide. RESULTS indexes exact claims and their proof or check records. Keep a problem-specific citation and any required notices. Formalized packages retain their own pinned Lake environment; different packages need not share a compiler pin.

Before admission:

1. Freeze exact statements, hypotheses and quantitative ranges. Distinguish the original problem from partial results and conditional statements.
2. Include the licensed proof dependency closure and retain source provenance. Exclude credentials, confidential materials and unlicensed paper copies.
3. Compare relevant eligible public sources freshly and record versions, exact theorem differences and reading limits. Preserve unresolved priority questions.
4. Review proof correctness and statement fidelity independently. For each new or changed theorem claim, require the exact declaration and complete proof dependency closure, the applicable official `check.sh` result ending in `RESULT: PASS`, an allowed-axiom audit, no `sorry` or `native_decide`, independent Lean kernel replay of the affected modules and dependencies, and independent exact-source statement-fidelity review. A supporting lemma or conditional endpoint does not verify a stronger conclusion. For computations, specify coverage, assumptions and replication; finite computational evidence alone does not admit an all-orders theorem.
5. Register only the released package in the collection catalogue and its applicable checks. Run metadata, link and export-boundary checks at final paths. Bind the exported mathematical inputs to authenticated complete Lean evidence; reuse accepted evidence when those inputs and coverage are unchanged. Verify changed mathematical inputs and their affected dependent scope. Preserve unchanged historical evidence labels and computational material at their recorded scope. New or changed theorem claims without full verification remain unreleased candidates; another package’s Lean checks cannot verify them.
6. Submit the exact package and public wording for maintainer review. Preserve historical evidence and the exact release state.

Changes to shared verification logic must run every affected package. Avoid moving mathematical claims into the root catalogue: the package is authoritative for their scope and evidence.
